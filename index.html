1 · Verb patterns: choose what comes next

gerund · to-infinitive · base form

Many exam items test the structure that follows a verb.

The main challenge is not the meaning of the first verb, but the pattern it requires after it.

PatternTypical verbsExample
verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, finish, suggest, considerShe enjoys reading.
verb + to + basewant, decide, plan, hope, needThey decided to leave.
verb + object + to + baseask, tell, want, adviseHe asked me to wait.
modal + basecan, should, must, mightYou should study.
enjoydoing
decideto do
ask someoneto do
modaldo
Common trap: Do not add to or -ing just because it sounds familiar. The controlling verb decides the pattern.
Exam strategy: Identify the first verb, then recall the pattern it normally takes before reading the distractors.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. She enjoys ___ new languages.
2. They decided ___ early.
3. The teacher asked us ___ quietly.

2 · Verb + gerund

enjoy · avoid · finish · suggest · consider · mind

Some verbs are followed by the -ing form.

verb + gerund (-ing)
I enjoy learning English.
She avoids driving at night.
They finished preparing the report.
He suggested taking a taxi.

High-priority verbs

enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest, consider, mind, keep, practice commonly take a gerund.

Common mistakes: enjoy to read ✗ · suggest to go ✗ → enjoy reading, suggest going.
Exam strategy: If the first verb belongs to a known gerund group, eliminate infinitive options immediately.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. He avoids ___ during rush hour.
2. We finished ___ the presentation.
3. She suggested ___ earlier.

3 · Verb + to-infinitive

want · decide · plan · hope · need · expect

Many verbs are followed by to + base verb.

verb + to + base verb
I want to improve my English.
We plan to travel in December.
She decided to apply.
They hope to finish soon.

Object + infinitive patterns

Some verbs often include an object before the infinitive: ask someone to..., tell someone to..., want someone to..., advise someone to....

Common mistakes: decide going ✗ · tell me go ✗ → decide to go, tell me to go.
Exam strategy: Look for an object before the blank. It may signal object + to-infinitive.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. We plan ___ next month.
2. She told me ___ the door.
3. They hope ___ the project soon.

4 · Gerund or infinitive? Meaning can change

remember · forget · stop · try

Some verbs can take both forms, but the meaning changes.

VerbGerund meaningInfinitive meaning
rememberremember a past actionremember something you need to do
forgetforget a past experience/actionfail to do something
stopend an activitypause one activity in order to do another
tryexperiment with a possible solutionmake an effort
I remember meeting her.
Remember to call her.
He stopped smoking.
He stopped to smoke.
Common trap: Both forms may be grammatically possible, so the context—not only the verb—decides the correct answer.
Exam strategy: Ask whether the sentence refers to a previous action, a future responsibility, ending an activity, or pausing to do something else.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. Remember ___ the lights before you leave.
2. I remember ___ that movie years ago.
3. He stopped ___ because the doctor advised him to.

5 · Defining Relative Clauses

who · which · that · where

A defining relative clause identifies exactly which person or thing we mean.

WordReferenceExample
whopeopleThe teacher who helped me was very patient.
whichthingsThe book which I bought is useful.
thatpeople or things in defining clausesThe course that I chose starts Monday.
whereplacesThat's the office where I work.

No commas

Because the information is essential, defining relative clauses are not separated by commas.

Common trap: Do not use where for a person or who for a thing.
Exam strategy: Identify the noun immediately before the blank: person, thing, or place.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. The woman ___ works here is my aunt.
2. The phone ___ I bought is already broken.
3. This is the café ___ we met.

6 · Non-defining Relative Clauses

extra information · commas · no that

A non-defining clause adds extra information about an already identified noun.

My brother, who lives in Cusco, is visiting us.
Lima, which is Peru's capital, is very large.
Mr. Lee, whose office is upstairs, is the manager.
The hotel, where we stayed, was excellent.

Commas matter.

The extra information is separated by commas. In standard exam grammar, that is not used in non-defining relative clauses.

Common mistakes: My father, that works here,... ✗ · Missing commas around non-essential information.
Exam strategy: Ask whether the noun is already fully identified. If yes and the clause only adds extra information, use commas and avoid that.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. My uncle, ___ lives in Arequipa, is a doctor.
2. This laptop, ___ I bought last year, still works well.
3. Which sentence is correct?

7 · Relative words, omission & reference

whose · where · when · omitted object pronouns

Relative words express different relationships.

WordUseExample
whosepossessionThe student whose laptop was lost...
whereplaceThe city where I grew up...
whentimeThe day when we met...

Object relative pronouns can sometimes disappear.

In defining clauses, if the relative word is the object, it can often be omitted: The book (that) I bought.... If it is the subject, it cannot be omitted: The man who called....

Common trap: Do not omit the relative pronoun when it is the subject of the relative clause.
Exam strategy: Look inside the relative clause. If there is already a subject after the blank, the relative word may be the object and may be omissible.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. The woman ___ car was stolen called the police.
2. The book ___ I borrowed was excellent.
3. Which relative word can be omitted in 'The movie ___ we watched was great'?

8 · Linking words: reason & result

because · because of · so · therefore

Choose the linker according to both meaning and grammar.

LinkerWhat followsExample
becauseclauseWe left because it was late.
because ofnoun / noun phraseWe left because of the weather.
soresult clauseIt was late, so we left.
thereforeresult, more formalIt was late; therefore, we left.
Common trap: because of it was raining ✗ · because the rain ✗
Exam strategy: Check what comes after the linker: a full clause or a noun phrase?

Quick Check · 3 items

1. We stayed home ___ it was raining.
2. The flight was canceled ___ bad weather.
3. It was raining, ___ we stayed home.

9 · Linking words: contrast & concession

although · even though · however · despite · in spite of

Contrast words have different grammar patterns.

LinkerPatternExample
although / even though+ clauseAlthough it was late, we continued.
howeverconnects separate clauses/sentencesIt was late. However, we continued.
despite / in spite of+ noun / gerundDespite the rain, we went out.
Although he was tired, he finished.
Despite being tired, he finished.
He was tired. However, he finished.
In spite of the delay, we arrived on time.
Common mistakes: despite he was tired ✗ · although the rain ✗
Exam strategy: Clause → although/even though. Noun or gerund → despite/in spite of.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. ___ she was tired, she finished the report.
2. ___ the traffic, we arrived on time.
3. The task was difficult. ___, we completed it.

10 · Question tags & sentence connection

auxiliary agreement · reference words · logical connection

Question tags repeat the sentence's auxiliary and reverse the polarity.

StatementTag
You're ready,aren't you?
She doesn't work here,does she?
They went home,didn't they?
He can swim,can't he?

Sentence connection & reference

In reading and grammar items, words such as this, that, these, those, it, they, however, therefore connect ideas. Their meaning depends on what came before.

The company changed its policy. This caused confusion.
The instructions were unclear. Therefore, several people made mistakes.
Common trap: A positive statement normally takes a negative tag, and a negative statement takes a positive tag.
Exam strategy: For tags, identify the auxiliary and subject. For reference words, ask exactly what earlier noun or idea the word points back to.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. You're coming tomorrow, ___?
2. She didn't call, ___?
3. The new rule created confusion. ___ was discussed at the meeting.
Week 8 exam habit: Do not answer from vocabulary alone. Identify the pattern: what does the first verb require, what noun does the relative clause refer to, and what logical relationship connects the two ideas?

Vocabulary & exam context

These structures often appear inside work, education, travel, communication and reading contexts. Use the vocabulary to understand the situation, but make the grammar decision from the structure.
